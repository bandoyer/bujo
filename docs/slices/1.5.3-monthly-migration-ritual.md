# Slice 1.5.3 — Monthly Migration ritual

Status: **operator-approved 2026-08-26; implementation contract.**

This slice adds the smallest guided Monthly Migration that preserves Ryder
Carroll's deliberate friction. It sets up one target Monthly Log, reviews the
previous month's unresolved tasks one at a time in their original tree
context, then scans the target month's due Future Log queue. It is a temporary
reference lens over existing Entry rows, never a new residency, container, or
automatic rollover.

The governing authority is `docs/METHOD.md`, then `PLAN.md`, then
`ARCHITECTURE.md`, followed by the landed 1.5.1/1.5.1a and 1.5.2 contracts.
The source check also returned to *The Bullet Journal Method* sections
"Tasks Page (or Mental Inventory)," "The Future Log," "Migration," "Monthly
Migration," and "Set Up Your Bullet Journal." The source requires a fresh
mental inventory, deliberate assessment of each incomplete task, three
destinations for worthy work, and a final Future Log scan. It does not specify
URLs, persisted wizard state, digital resume behavior, or a precise clock
cutoff for "the last couple days"; the approved digital translations are
fixed at the end of this document.

## Acceptance contract

- Monthly Migration is an explicitly opened ritual for one **target month**.
  Its outgoing source is exactly the preceding calendar month. Opening a
  month, crossing midnight, or viewing any log never starts it or moves data.
- Setup offers a task-only rapid-log surface for the target Monthly Tasks page
  and continues through `Entry.capture!`. An empty setup is valid; the app
  never invents a Monthly Log row, starter task, or completion record.
- The outgoing review references every kept, rendered, open task resident of
  the source month's Monthly Calendar, Monthly Tasks, and Daily Logs. Each task
  is considered once at a time with its complete kept root tree visible.
- One candidate can be struck, carried to the target Monthly Tasks page,
  moved to a known exact-Topic Custom Collection, or scheduled beyond the
  target month into the Future Log. There is no Keep, Skip, Select all, or
  automatic recommendation.
- After the outgoing set is resolved, the Future scan references unmoved kept
  root tasks and events whose `occurs_on` falls inside the target month. Tasks
  move to target Monthly Tasks; events move to the target Monthly Calendar.
- Every movement remains one append-only `Entry#move_to!` successor. Nothing
  changes an Entry's placement in place, reparents children, copies a tree, or
  writes a second successor.
- The ritual has no progress table, status column, session row, or completion
  flag. Remaining work is derived from current kept rows, task state, and
  successor existence. Leaving and reopening recomputes the truthful next
  candidate.
- Ordinary Monthly and Future screens keep their current command rules. In
  particular, Future residents gain no general-purpose row controls; only a
  currently eligible ritual candidate may use the new ritual endpoints.
- UUIDv7 ids, exact NULL state for events and notes, immutable residency,
  same-user validation, soft deletion without cascades, the unique successor
  chain, and dormant `hlc`/`server_seq` remain binding.

## Source boundary — what the ritual means

The ritual preserves the source sequence:

1. Prepare the new Monthly Log and unload a fresh mental inventory onto its
   Tasks page.
2. Scan the preceding month's Monthly and Daily pages. For each incomplete
   task, decide whether it still matters and is still vital.
3. Strike an irrelevant task. Rewrite a worthy task into the new Monthly Tasks
   page or a relevant Custom Collection, or schedule a date-specific task
   beyond the new month into the Future Log.
4. Scan the Future Log for the new month. Rewrite due tasks into Monthly Tasks
   and due events into the Calendar.

The app's accepted successor chain is the digital body of rewriting: the
predecessor remains visible with `>` or `<`, and the successor is a fresh root.
The app does not add typing friction on top of that settled translation, but it
also does not collapse the assessment into a batch operation.

Custom Collections are possible destinations, not another source set. Their
open tasks are reviewed only when the reader deliberately opens that
Collection. Yearly/notebook migration and Collection sustainability review
remain separate future work.

## No schema and no ritual entity

This slice adds no table, column, migration, seed, or synced entity.

A Monthly Log already exists as two Entry residency scopes for a month. The
setup gesture writes only actual task Entries. The review screen references
Entries where they currently reside; it does not confer membership in a
"migration" and does not write a queue position. Completion means only that
the current derived candidate relations are empty.

Consequences of that minimal design are deliberate:

- reopening a partially completed ritual resumes at the first still-eligible
  candidate;
- retrying or refreshing never duplicates an action because a resolved task
  is no longer open or already has a successor;
- a new source-month open task added after an earlier completion appears when
  the ritual is opened again;
- a task changed in another tab is re-evaluated rather than trusted from stale
  page state;
- there is no permanent "month migrated" badge, history dashboard, percentage,
  streak, reminder, or notification.

The existing Entry and Collection rows remain the only sync-sensitive journal
state. This slice never writes `hlc` or `server_seq`.

## Target month and availability

The canonical ritual URL names an exact target month as `YYYY-MM`. The server
derives the source month as `target_month.prev_month`; a request never supplies
an independent source month or destination `page_on`.

The proposed v1 admission rule is:

- any past or current target month may be opened for deliberate catch-up;
- the immediately next month may also be opened, allowing the book's
  month-end setup without inventing an hour or "last couple days" cutoff;
- a target later than next month is absent from the UI and a crafted request is
  refused without rendering or writing journal data.

The Monthly Log shows one **Monthly Migration** link for an admitted target
month. Current and past target pages are already ordinary writing surfaces;
next month's page remains ordinary-capture closed, but its ritual setup form is
available through this one explicit path. Merely visiting next month's normal
Calendar or Tasks page still offers no capture control.

Malformed months, impossible months, missing months, and too-far-future months
render one Month-chrome HTTP 404 state titled `Monthly Migration not found`.
They do not silently fall back to the current month because this URL can lead
to writes.

Every controller snapshots `Time.zone.today` once. Models receive explicit
`as_of:` and `target_month:` inputs; no model reads the clock.

## Stage 1 — fresh mental inventory

The ritual opens on **Set up the month**. The screen shows the kept resident
trees already on the target Monthly Tasks page, followed by a task-only
rapid-log surface and a deliberate **Review outgoing tasks** action.

The form uses the shared parser and `Entry.capture!` with:

```ruby
user:       Current.user
today:      @today
as_of:      @today
page_kind:  "monthly_tasks"
page_on:    @target_month
```

It also carries a named monthly-migration admission context that may open only
this derived target Monthly Tasks page. That context belongs at the domain
boundary; the controller may not bypass admission with `save!`, mass
assignment, or raw creation. `Entry.capture!` remains the only parser-to-row
bridge.

The parser remains frozen. Explicit event or note roots are structurally
refused; a blank line is a no-op. Optional parsed `occurs_on`, time, priority,
and tags keep their current Monthly Tasks meanings. Success and refusal remain
on setup; refusal preserves the typed line. Capture creates no Calendar row,
does not migrate an old task, and does not imply that setup is complete.

The reader may add no fresh tasks and continue. Setup is an invitation to
offload what matters, not a required starter item or validation gate.
The outgoing-review URL is therefore also a valid direct Continue gesture;
there is no hidden setup flag for it to bypass.

## Stage 2 — outgoing task review

### Exact candidate set

The source-month candidate set is every kept task with `state: "open"`, no
successor, and an id inside the trees the source pages actually render:

- roots from `monthly_calendar(source_month)` and all recursively kept
  descendants;
- roots from `monthly_tasks(source_month)` and all recursively kept
  descendants;
- roots from each `daily_log(day)` for every day in the source month and all
  recursively kept descendants.

This includes nested open tasks because the plan requires every unresolved
task in tree context. It excludes events, notes, done/struck/migrated tasks,
soft-deleted rows, descendants hidden beneath a non-kept ancestor, any row
with a successor, Future residents, Collection residents, and every other
month. `occurs_on` never grants membership.

Candidates use paper page order, made deterministic for digital resume:

1. Monthly Calendar trees in calendar/capture order;
2. Monthly Tasks trees in capture order;
3. Daily Log trees from the first day through the last day, each in capture
   order;
4. inside one tree, depth-first pre-order using each sibling relation's
   existing capture order and UUIDv7 `id` tiebreaker.

The first eligible id is the current candidate. The server recomputes this
order for every GET and mutation; the client never submits a next id or queue
position.

### Tree context

The page renders the candidate's complete kept resident root tree, not the
whole source page. The candidate row is visibly identified as `Review this
task`; its ancestors, siblings, and descendants remain readable context.
Context rows expose no lifecycle or movement controls inside this lens. Other
open tasks in the same tree receive their own turn later.

Moving a root or nested candidate keeps the existing contract: only that row
is copied into one fresh destination root. Its parent and children stay in the
source tree unchanged. A later child candidate can therefore be assessed on
its own merits.

The source label is derived from persisted residency and appears below the
page title: `Monthly Calendar · August 2026`, `Monthly Tasks · August 2026`,
or `Daily Log · August 14, 2026`. Request parameters never author that label.

### Candidate gestures

An outgoing candidate offers exactly four resolutions:

| Gesture | Result |
|---|---|
| **Strike** | `strike!`; the task stays on its source page as irrelevant |
| **Move to September Tasks** | append one root on target Monthly Tasks |
| **Move to Collection…** | reveal one exact-Topic field, resolve inside the current user's kept Collections, then append one Collection root |
| **Schedule in Future…** | reveal one date field; require a date after the target month's end, then append one Future root |

There is no Complete/Reopen inside this lens, no Skip/Keep here, no free-form
month picker, no Collection list or autocomplete, and no automatic destination
based on tags, dates, priority, or text. The reader can leave through normal
navigation and resolve the task on its source page; reopening the ritual then
recomputes eligibility.

Move to target Tasks preserves the current Monthly movement contract: text,
priority, tags, kind, and user copy; the successor is an open root; optional
source `occurs_on` and `time_of_day` are cleared while the predecessor retains
them. Move to Collection reuses 1.5.2's exact case-insensitive Topic equality,
clears dates/time, and never indexes or creates a Collection. Schedule keeps
the chosen `occurs_on` and carries source time under the existing Future
movement rule.

The Future-date horizon is the **target month**, not merely the wall-clock
month. While setting up September during August, September 30 is not a Future
destination; October 1 is. The domain operation receives the target month
explicitly and enforces this boundary before writing.

## Stage 3 — due Future scan

The Future stage is reachable only after the outgoing candidate relation is
empty. A direct or stale request while outgoing tasks remain redirects to the
first outgoing candidate without writing.

The exact due set is `Current.user.entries.future_log` roots whose own
`occurs_on` falls inside the target month, whose row is kept, and which have no
successor:

- an open task is eligible;
- an event with exact NULL state is eligible;
- done, struck, or migrated tasks are not eligible;
- notes are structurally impossible as Future roots;
- overdue months and later months are not silently swept into this target.

Exact-month scoping is intentional. A missed earlier item remains visible on
the Future Log and belongs to the catch-up ritual for its own target month;
this ritual does not silently reinterpret an old event's calendar date.

Candidates order by `occurs_on`, then `time_of_day` with NULL last, then
capture order and `id`. Each candidate renders its complete kept root tree as
read-only context.

The Future command matrix is:

| Candidate | Controls | Successor |
|---|---|---|
| open task | **Move to September Tasks**, **Strike** | moving creates an open target Monthly Tasks root; striking writes no successor |
| event, NULL state | **Move to September Calendar** | creates a target Monthly Calendar root with the same `occurs_on` and `time_of_day` |
| any other Future row/state | none | every crafted ritual command refuses |

A moved Future task follows the existing Monthly Tasks rewrite contract and
clears `occurs_on`/`time_of_day` on the successor; the dated predecessor keeps
them. Its predecessor becomes `migrated` and renders `>`. A moved Future event
keeps NULL state on both rows, gains one successor, and renders `>` because the
successor resides on a Monthly page. Priority, tags, text, and user copy in
both cases; children never follow.

Strike is available only to an eligible Future **task** inside this ritual.
Future events have no invented irrelevant/deleted state. The ordinary Future
Log screen remains read-only, and its existing six generic crafted Entry
commands remain refused.

When no Future candidate remains, the ritual renders **Monthly Migration
complete** with links to the target Calendar and Tasks pages. This is a live
empty-state statement, not a persisted completion. If eligible source work is
later added, reopening returns to that work.

Future and completion URLs always canonicalize to the earliest unresolved
stage. A copied completion URL cannot skip a newly added outgoing or Future
candidate.

## Complete ritual-command authorization

The existing six generic Entry commands keep the 1.5.2 residency matrix. This
slice does not add `future` to any generic command allowlist.

New ritual endpoints apply this independent, narrower matrix after a
current-user kept lookup and candidate-membership check:

| Ritual command | Outgoing open task in source set | Due Future open task | Due Future event | Everything else |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Strike | allow | allow | refuse | refuse |
| Carry to target Monthly Tasks | allow | allow | refuse | refuse |
| Move to exact-Topic Collection | allow | refuse | refuse | refuse |
| Schedule beyond target month | allow | refuse | refuse | refuse |
| Move to target Monthly Calendar | refuse | refuse | allow | refuse |

The matrix is only the ritual/residency gate. `Entry` still owns kind, state,
successor, structural destination, same-user Collection, and append-only
lifecycle validation. A rendered control and a crafted request must reach the
same domain rule, but absence of a control is never authorization.

The server derives target placement from the canonical target month and the
command. It ignores or refuses crafted `source_month`, `target_month`,
`page_kind`, `page_on`, `collection_id`, `return_to`, `viewed_on`,
`occurs_on` on non-date commands, and entry ownership parameters. The only
reader-authored destination values are the exact Topic for the Collection
gesture and the ISO date for Schedule in Future.

## Success, refusal, stale, and missing destinations

- Setup capture success/refusal returns to setup; a validation refusal is
  rendered with the submitted line intact.
- An outgoing action success recomputes the relation and shows the next
  outgoing candidate, or advances to the Future stage when none remain.
- A Future action success recomputes and shows the next due candidate, or the
  complete state when none remain.
- A lifecycle, destination, wrong-stage, or structurally ineligible refusal
  leaves every row unchanged and returns to the same current candidate with
  `REFUSAL_ALERT`. Exact-Topic misses disclose no candidate or tenant detail.
- If the signed-in user's row still exists but another tab already resolved it,
  the stale request performs no write, shows the normal refusal, and redirects
  to the newly recomputed current stage rather than trapping the reader on an
  obsolete id.
- A missing, foreign, or soft-deleted entry id returns one HTTP 404 page titled
  `Migration item not found`, with Month active and no requested id, Topic,
  entry text, or tenant distinction. A foreign Collection destination remains
  an ordinary exact-Topic miss because the source candidate itself exists.

Every write is one transaction. A refusal reload-proves unchanged predecessor,
no successor, unchanged target pages, unchanged Collections, and untouched
sync placeholders.

## Routes and controller boundaries

Use a normal `MonthlyMigrationsController` (or Rails-equivalent singular
controller) plus named target-month routes. There is no `MonthlyMigration`
model and no service/policy layer.

The route surface distinguishes these gestures:

- `GET` setup for one target month;
- `POST` one fresh-inventory task line;
- `GET` outgoing review and `POST` each of its four resolutions;
- `GET` Future scan and `POST` its task/event resolutions;
- `GET` live completion state, or the Future route rendering completion once
  empty.

Exact Rails helper names are implementation choices; separate HTTP verbs and
server-derived destinations are behavior. All routes require authentication.

Every query starts from `Current.user.entries` or
`Current.user.collections`. Candidate membership is re-derived on the server
for the named target month before any action body. No controller uses unscoped
`Entry.find`, `Collection.find`, a request-authored user id, or an ids array
posted by the browser.

## Smallest usable phone interface

Future, and Index remain usable exit routes; leaving never mutates or confirms
anything.

Every state follows the landed hierarchy rule: **`Monthly Migration` is the
first visible text and first heading.** Context such as
`August → September 2026`, `Set up the month`, `Review outgoing tasks`, or
`Scan the Future Log` follows underneath. No eyebrow, step number, progress
bar, or status badge appears above the title.
Month names in this document are illustrative; rendered copy always derives
them from the canonical target and source months.

### Setup state

- Title first, then `August → September 2026` and `Fresh mental inventory`.
- Existing target Tasks trees render read-only in capture order.
- Empty copy: `Nothing in September Tasks yet.`
- One task-only rapid-log field with `What matters this month?` as its visible
  label, a 44px `Log task` action, and the existing kind/glyph grammar.
- One 44px `Review outgoing tasks` action. No required task count and no
  automatic Continue after capture.

### Outgoing candidate state

- Title first, then month context and `Review outgoing tasks`.
- One source label and one complete tree, with the candidate row visibly
  selected and labelled for assistive technology.
- Four 44px actions. Collection and Future choices reveal one second step at a
  time using the existing action-strip vocabulary: exact Topic + Move, or date

### Future candidate and complete states

- Title first, then month context and `Scan the Future Log`.
- One due root tree. A task shows Move to Tasks and Strike; an event shows Move

- Empty Future stage becomes `Monthly Migration complete` with quiet 44px

## Ruled browser flows

1. From an admitted target Monthly Log, the reader opens Monthly Migration and
   sees setup with the title first. A next-month target accepts a task through
   this ritual while the normal next-month Tasks page remains capture-closed.
2. Fresh-inventory task capture preserves parser semantics, UUIDv7, placement,
   and dormant sync fields. Blank/event/note lines create no invalid row and
   remain on setup with readable refusal behavior.
3. The outgoing stage presents Monthly Calendar, Monthly Tasks, then Daily
   candidates in the ruled tree order. A nested task appears selected inside
   its complete kept root tree; context rows have no controls.
4. Strike resolves one outgoing task and presents the next. Leaving and
   reopening resumes from derived state; no progress row or duplicate write is
   created.
5. Move to target Tasks creates one root successor, marks the predecessor
   migrated, clears temporal fields on the successor, preserves words and
   source tree, renders `>`, and stays inside the ritual.
6. Move to Collection accepts only a complete kept same-user Topic, never
   lists candidates, never indexes the destination, and treats blank, wrong,
   deleted, and foreign-only Topics identically.
7. Schedule in Future refuses the target month's last day and accepts the next
   day after it, creates one Future successor with `<`, and never trusts a
   crafted target placement.
8. The Future stage cannot be skipped while outgoing candidates remain. Once
   reached, only target-month roots appear; overdue and later Future residents
   remain on their own queue months.
9. A due Future task can move to target Tasks or be struck. A due Future event
   moves to the target Calendar with its date/time. Both are append-only,
   return to the ritual, and leave children on the predecessor.
10. Resolving the last due item renders the live complete state with target
    Calendar/Tasks links. Adding a new eligible source task later makes the
    ritual show work again rather than preserving a false completion flag.
11. Wrong state, second move, stale tab, malformed target, far-future target,
    soft-deleted row, and crafted destination/referrer parameters refuse with
    the ruled return and zero unrelated writes.
12. Another user's target ids, Entry ids, Collection ids/Topics, source words,
    and successors remain undisclosed and unchanged across every GET and POST.
13. Setup, nested outgoing review, both second steps, Future task/event, and
    completion are exercised at phone width in both themes and at least two
    hands. Existing Daily, Monthly, Future, Collection, Index, command, and
    placement flows remain green.

## Approved digital translations

The book settles the deliberate sequence and the three outgoing destinations,
but not these digital translations. The operator approved all five on
2026-08-26; they are binding parts of this implementation contract:

1. **Month admission:** target month is the URL authority; any past/current
   target plus the immediately next month is allowed, with no guessed
   last-days cutoff and no farther-future setup.
2. **Derived progress:** there is no ritual/completion row. Resume, stage
   advancement, and completion are recomputed from kept Entry state and
   successor links, so later-added unresolved work can reappear.
3. **Review order and tree scope:** Monthly Calendar → Monthly Tasks → Daily
   Logs in day order, then depth-first capture order; every rendered nested open
   task receives its own turn inside its full root tree.
4. **Future boundary and controls:** only Future roots dated inside the exact
   target month are scanned; due open tasks may Move to Tasks or Strike, while
   due events may only Move to Calendar. Ordinary Future pages stay read-only.
5. **Due-task temporal fields:** a Future task rewritten to Monthly Tasks uses
   the existing Monthly rewrite contract and clears `occurs_on`/time on the
   successor while preserving them on the predecessor; a Future event moved to
   Calendar preserves both.

Any later change to one of these five decisions requires an explicit contract
amendment rather than an implementation-time guess.

The pre-implementation audit checked this contract against the landed code
(`Entry`, `Collection`, `EntryCommandAuthorization`, `JournalReading`, the
Monthly/Future/Entries controllers) and against the approved mock. Nothing
below changes a product decision or reopens the five approved translations;
these lines only resolve precedence and record code alignment so no downstream
role has to guess.

1. **Copy precedence.** Where this contract quotes a literal string —
   `Review outgoing tasks`, `Scan the Future Log`, `Fresh mental inventory`,
   `What matters this month?`, `Log task`, `Nothing in September Tasks yet.`,
   `Review this task`, `Monthly Migration complete`,
   `Monthly Migration not found`, `Migration item not found`, the
   `August → September 2026` context form, and the source-label forms — the
   contract's string is normative. The mock binds hierarchy, layout, spacing,
   tokens, and theme treatment; its variant or abbreviated copy
   (`Review open tasks`, `August → September`, `Bring September into view`,
   `Migration complete.`, `Monthly task` / `Add`) is illustrative only.
   The four resolution buttons may use the compact labels both the contract's
   phone grid and the mock render (`Strike`, `September Tasks`,
   `Collection…`, `Future…`) provided each control's accessible name states
   the full gesture unambiguously.
2. **Event glyph.** The mock's `O` is a placeholder. Rendered glyphs follow
   the landed `Entry#glyph` vocabulary (`•`, `x`, `>`, `<`, `○`, `–`) and the
   project article; no glyph code changes.
3. **Future-horizon input.** `Entry#move_to!` already guards a Future
   destination against a caller-supplied boundary date's month end. The
   observable rule is what binds: during this ritual the boundary is the
   target month (refuse the target month's last day, accept the day after),
   never the wall-clock month. Whether the coder supplies the target month
   through the existing boundary input or an explicitly named target-month
   input is an implementation choice; the controller still snapshots
   `Time.zone.today` once and models still read no clock.
4. **Setup admission context.** The named monthly-migration admission context
   widens `Entry` capture admission only for the derived target Monthly Tasks
   page reached through this ritual. `Entry.capture_admitted?` as asked by the
   ordinary Monthly/Future screens, `MonthlyLogsController`'s `@capture_open`,
   and generic `EntriesController#create` behavior must answer exactly as they
   do today — including refusing ordinary next-month Monthly Tasks capture.
5. **No schema confirmed.** Both candidate relations, tree context, resume,
   and completion are derivable from existing columns, `kept`, the
   `successor`/`predecessor` associations, and the landed recursive
   rendered-tree boundary. The audit found no schema need; a coder who
   believes otherwise must stop and report it as a blocker per this contract.
6. **Refusal copy.** Ritual refusals reuse the reader-facing
   `That entry can't do that.` alert (`REFUSAL_ALERT`) already established by
   the Entries commands, not a new message.
