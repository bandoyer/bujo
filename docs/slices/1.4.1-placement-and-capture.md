# Slice 1.4.1 — placement and capture

The capture bar learns the method's real move: **route by gesture,
not by parsing**. The bar hides at rest, appears when you tap the
empty space of the page you're on, and writes onto *that* page — and
the Future Log gains the notebook's own gesture, writing under a
month header. Per the operator-picked "Write on the page" and "Add
under a month" artboards on the
[Placement Gestures canvas](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/9299895f-f328-4910-99e3-2fe3754b4f40)
("Place from today" is deliberately NOT in this slice).

> **Partly superseded by 1.5.1.** Daily capture is now limited to
> today and past pages (the source's night-before exception is
> consciously deferred). A Future month-add creates Future Log
> residency only for dates after the current month; it no longer
> creates Daily/Calendar membership or uses the merely-after-today
> boundary. The remaining gesture and parser rulings below stand.

**This spec pre-decides the judgment calls; report gaps instead of
inventing rulings.**

## Ruling: this supersedes 1.3's capture-always-logs-into-today

The 1.3 ruling guarded against the bar *accidentally* following the
viewed day. This slice replaces it deliberately: **capture logs onto
the page you opened.** Navigating to a day and writing on it is the
analog method's own gesture — turning to the page IS the intention.
The Daily Log form always carries its page's date; nothing is
inferred.

Consequences, ruled:

- `EntriesController#create` accepts an `on` ISO-date param. The
  capture form posts it server-rendered (the viewed day). Absent or
  unparseable `on` refuses via the existing rejected-action path —
  never a 500/400, and never a silent fallback to today (a capture
  landing on the wrong page is worse than one that asks again).
- The captured entry's `logged_on` is the page date, and **the
  parser's `today:` argument is the page date too** — a relative
  token typed on the SEP 5 page (`tomorrow`, `friday`) resolves
  against SEP 5. The page is "today" while you write on it. One
  temporal context per capture, no mixing.
- `Entry.capture!` already takes `today:`; the model layer does not
  change.

## The hidden bar (Daily Log, every day)

1. **At rest, no bar.** The page is journal only.
2. **The reveal control is the empty region below the entry list** —
   a real `<button>` (the whole area, min 44px tall, stretching to
   fill remaining space; `aria-label` "Write on this page"), visually
   nothing at all. Tapping it reveals the bar (existing anatomy: kind
   circles, input, submit) with the input focused. Tapping the region
   again hides it.
- 3. **After a successful capture the bar hides again.** One bullet,
   one gesture — matching the operator's ask. The Turbo Stream
   append still lands the new row.
4. **The page cue**: when the viewed day is not the real today, a
   small mono accent line above the bar reads `→ logging to SEP 5`
   (the page's date, computed). On today, no cue.
5. **The submit button's copy becomes `Log`** — "Log entry" reads
   like a database. Tests select the new exact label.
6. Reveal state is client-only (the 1.3.1 rules): a Stimulus
   controller, `aria-expanded` on the control, `hidden` on the bar,
   nothing persisted.

## Write under a month (Future Log)

1. Each month header (the six-month runway and any occupied later
   month) becomes a **toggle button** (≥ 44px hit target, the header
   text unchanged visually, `aria-expanded`).
2. Tapping reveals an inline add row directly beneath the header:
   a bare **day-number field** (numeric, the mock shows just `5` —
   no label text beyond its `aria-label` "Day of the month"), the
   text input, and a `Log` submit. One month's row open at a time,
   reveal client-only, hidden after a successful add.
3. Submitting posts the same `entries#create` with `on` composed
   from that month + the day. **Validity is refused, not corrected**:
   a day outside the month (`32`, Feb 30) takes the rejected-action
   path with the alert; the row stays available.
4. The created entry gets `logged_on` = `occurs_on` = that date —
   it lives on that day's page (it will simply *be there* when the
   day arrives), appears under the month in the Future Log, and on
   the Monthly calendar. The text runs through the parser exactly
   like any capture, with `today:` = that date; explicit glyph
   prefixes work; there are no kind circles in this row (default
   kind task; the mock keeps it minimal).

## Day navigation polish (rides along — same header, same tests)

## Ruled flows (system lane; 1.3 browser rules bind)

1. Today at rest shows no bar; tapping the empty region reveals it
   focused; capturing appends the row and hides the bar; the button
   reads `Log`.
2. Tapping the region again (without capturing) hides the bar.
3. Navigate to another day: the cue names that page; a capture lands
   on it (visible there after reload, absent from today's log); the
   nav center shows the viewed day and no "today" link exists; the
   Today tab returns home.
4. A relative token captured on a future page resolves against that
   page (capture `friday` on a page and assert the computed date).
5. On the Future Log, tapping a month header reveals the add row;
   day + text lands under that month and on that day's calendar;
   the row hides after; a second header closes the first.
6. An invalid day number refuses with the alert and the entry does
   not exist.
7. Task actions, theme/hand toggles, and the tab bar still work with
   the bar hidden (no regression in the reveal families coexisting).

Fast lane: `on` param set/absent/invalid on create (including the
refuse-not-fallback rule), `logged_on` = `occurs_on` for month adds,
parser `today:` receiving the page date, auth unchanged, and the
existing capture tests updated to post `on` explicitly rather than
weakened.

### Ruling: the page date reaches everything downstream of a capture

"Capture logs onto the page you opened" is not one substitution. Today
`EntriesController#create` computes `@today = Time.zone.today` and feeds
it to four separate things, and every one of them must become the page
date or the capture is only half-placed:

1. the parser's `today:` argument — ruled above;
2. `logged_on`, via the same argument;
3. `open_task_count_on(...)`, so the header count belongs to the page
   being written on;
4. the appended row's `viewed_on` local in `create.turbo_stream.erb`,
   **and** the HTML fallback's `redirect_to daily_log_path(...)`.

Item 4 is the one that hides. `viewed_on` is what a row's action forms
post back, and `migrate` computes `viewed_date.next_month`. A row
captured onto SEP 5 but appended with today's `viewed_on` would look
correct and then migrate to the wrong month the first time anyone
pressed it — a wrong write, from a screen that rendered right. A browser
test that captures on a non-today page and then migrates that very row
catches it; asserting only that the row appeared does not.

### Ruling: a refused capture leaves the reader where they were writing

`refuse_lifecycle_change` redirects to `redirect_to_viewed_day`, which
derives its day from `params[:viewed_on]` and falls back to today. The
Future Log's month-add row posts no `viewed_on`, so a refused day
number — the exact case flow 6 exercises — would bounce the reader from
the Future Log to today's Daily Log. The alert would appear, the entry
would correctly not exist, and flow 6 as written would still pass while
the reader lost their place.

A refused capture returns the reader to the page they submitted from,
with the alert. Refusing a bad `on` follows the same rule: the
destination cannot be derived from the value being refused, so it comes
from the request rather than the param. Flow 6 asserts the reader is
still on the Future Log afterwards.

### Ruling: a capture's response must address the page it came from

`create.turbo_stream.erb` targets `empty_daily_log`, `entries`,
`open_task_count`, and `rapid_log_form` — every one a Daily Log id.
Turbo drops stream actions whose targets are absent **silently**, so
rendering that response to a Future Log month add produces no error, no
row, and no clue: the reader taps Log and nothing happens.

After a successful month add the reader stays on the Future Log and sees
the new entry under its month header. Whether that is a redirect or a
stream addressed to the Future Log's own ids is the coder's call; what
is ruled is that the response must address the page it was submitted
from, and that "nothing visibly happened" is a failure, not a no-op.

### Ruling: focus follows the bar, and lands on the control that opened it

1.3 ruled that focus stays in the input after a capture, and flow 2
asserts it. This slice hides the bar after a successful capture, so that
is no longer possible and the assertion must change rather than be
dropped: **focus returns to the reveal control** — the empty-region
button on the Daily Log, the month header on the Future Log — which is
by then back to `aria-expanded="false"`.

Without this the bar vanishes and focus falls to `<body>`, which loses a
keyboard reader's place entirely and is invisible to every assertion
that only checks what is displayed. Focus is managed by the reveal, not
by `autofocus:` on a field that now starts inside a hidden container.

### Ruling: the four existing tests that change, and how

Each is an update; a diff that shrinks any of them is a finding.

- **`capture` (system helper)** reveals the bar before filling, and its
  closing `assert_field "Rapid log…", with: ""` becomes an assertion
  that the bar is hidden — the new end state of the gesture.
- **Flow 2** keeps every row assertion; its focus check moves from the
  line field to the reveal control, per the ruling above.
- **Flow 7** inverts: the previous day now shows the bar, names the page
  in the cue, shows the viewed day between the chevrons, and offers no
  "today" link. It gains the capture-lands-here assertion; it does not
  merely drop `assert_no_field`.
- **Fast-lane capture tests** post `on` explicitly. The blank-line no-op
  keeps asserting a 200 and no row created; it now also carries a valid
  `on`, so that a future regression in `on` handling cannot be mistaken
  for the blank-line path.

The submit label changes from `Log entry` to `Log` everywhere it is
selected, exactly, and no other control gains a name containing it.

### Ruling: a live insert must read the way a reload reads

Raised by qa in verification pass 1, and the gap is this spec's. I ruled
that the response must address the page it came from and that "nothing
visibly happened" is a failure — but said nothing about *where* in the
page the new row lands, so an append was a faithful reading.

The Future Log is `in_calendar_order`. Appending a SEP 5 capture to a
month already showing SEP 20 and SEP 25 leaves the live list reading
20, 25, 5 until a reload silently reorders it to 5, 20, 25. The screen
tells the reader something the database does not agree with, and the
correction is invisible — nobody reloads to check an order they just
watched appear.

**After a month add, the month's entries read exactly as a reload would
render them.** The mechanism stays the coder's: re-rendering that
month's entries container (its id already exists, and the ordered
relation composes from `future_log` without a new scope) is the obvious
in-scope answer, and an append remains fine for the Daily Log, whose
list is in capture order and where a new capture genuinely does belong
last.

Flow 5 gains the case that discriminates it: with two later days already
under a month, add an earlier day and assert the live order before any
reload.

### Historical ruling: refuse a date the runway cannot show

> **Superseded by 1.5.1's page model.** The failure described here is
> why the server must guard the add row, but the replacement boundary
> is after the current month and the replacement residency is the
> Future Log alone.

Also from pass 1. On August 24, with the AUGUST header on screen because
something later in August is upcoming, its add row accepts day 1. The
entry is created, appended, and persisted with `logged_on` and
`occurs_on` of August 1 — and disappears on reload, because the Future
Log shows only `occurs_on > today`. A write that reports success and
then vanishes is the worst outcome available, and it is the same
principle this slice already ruled for the capture bar: a capture that
quietly lands somewhere the reader did not mean is worse than one that
asks again.

I defined validity for this row as "a real day in that month", which was
incomplete under 1.4.1's derived `future_log(after:)` scope. At the time,
the composed date also had to be after today or it vanished on reload.
The rejected-action path, open row after refusal, and server-side guard
remain the contract; 1.5.1 replaces the obsolete after-today rule with
after-current-month admission to a resident Future page.

**This restriction belongs to the Future Log's add row alone.** The
Daily Log capture bar must keep accepting past dates — writing on the
page you opened is this slice's entire point, and flow 3 captures onto a
previous day deliberately. A refusal applied to both would pass this
ruling's tests and break the slice's headline feature, so the fast lane
pins both sides: a past date refused under a month header, and the same
past date accepted from that day's own page.
