# Slice 1.5.3a — phone capture and ritual clarity

Status: **APPROVED 2026-08-26; implementation has not started.**

This is a focused usability amendment before 1.5.4 Daily Reflection. It
corrects places where the landed phone interface technically exposes the
approved gesture but does not make the intended writing surface reachable
where a reader naturally taps. It also makes an empty Monthly Migration end
only after a deliberate final gesture.

The authority remains `docs/METHOD.md`, then `PLAN.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`, and
the landed slice contracts. The operator's phone review on 2026-08-26 is the
dogfood evidence for this amendment. The review found implementation gaps in
1.4.1's whole-empty-region ruling and 1.5.1's Calendar-row ruling, and it
supersedes the affected phone details in 1.5.2 and 1.5.3. Nothing here changes
what a page means or lets the app act without a hand.

## Acceptance contract

- The Daily Log title appears once. Its viewed date remains in the centered
  day navigation and is removed from directly beneath `Daily Log`.
- On writable Daily Logs and Monthly Tasks pages, the writing surface begins
  immediately after the last rendered entry and fills the usable blank canvas
  above the fixed tab bar. A tap anywhere in that trailing blank canvas opens
  the existing capture form.
- A writable Monthly Calendar day has a primary, visible row gesture that
  opens capture for that exact Calendar date. Opening a row never creates an
  entry; only submitting the form does.
- A writable Future month opens its add form from the month heading or from
  the blank row immediately below its current last entry. Its Task/Event
  choice is an unmistakable two-option control, and every part of an entry
  shares one visual baseline.
- The Index retains `New Collection`, and the usable blank canvas below its
  content opens that same form. This creates no Collection, reveals no
  unindexed Topic, and performs no search until a form is submitted.
- An empty outgoing or Future stage is a truthful ritual checkpoint, not an
  automatic completion. The reader explicitly advances through both and
  explicitly finishes before seeing `Monthly Migration complete`.
- All reveal state remains browser-local and ephemeral. A reveal tap makes no
  journal write, creates no starter row, chooses no lifecycle action, and
  changes no migration progress state.
- Existing page residency, capture admission, command authorization, return
  destinations, tenant scoping, UUIDv7, append-only movement, soft deletion,
  and dormant `hlc`/`server_seq` behavior remain unchanged.

## Daily Log — one date and the actual rest of the page

The header is:

```text
Daily Log
‹                       WED · AUG 26                       ›
```

The single navigation row continues to identify the viewed day. The duplicate
`WED · AUG 26` under the title is absent in HTML, not merely hidden with CSS.
Screen readers therefore encounter the page date once in this header.

1. The entry list sizes to its rendered content; it does not reserve an inert
   minimum-height block beneath the last entry.
2. The existing real button labelled `Write on this page` starts immediately
   after that list and flexes to the tab bar. It remains visually blank and at
   least 44px high even on a full page where little space remains.
3. Tapping an entry or one of its action controls never bubbles into capture.
   Tapping any point below the last entry that is not another visible control
   opens capture and focuses `Rapid log…`.
4. Empty, one-entry, wrapped-entry, open-action-strip, refused-action-alert,
   today, and past-day states all keep the same boundary. A future Daily page
   remains capture-closed.
5. Successful and refused capture behavior is unchanged: success appends one
   resident and closes the form; refusal stays on the viewed page with the
   submitted line available according to the existing contract.

This clarifies rather than replaces 1.4.1: the approved reveal control was
always the whole empty region below the entry list. The landed `min-height` on
the list made part of that visibly empty region inert.

## Monthly Tasks — the same trailing writing surface

Current and past Monthly Tasks pages use the same geometry and interaction as
Daily capture:

- the resident list and `N open · M logged` copy consume only their content;
- the `Write on Monthly Tasks` button begins immediately afterward and fills
  the remaining page;
- any blank point in that region reveals the existing task-only form;
- Task remains the only rendered and server-admitted root kind;
- future Monthly Tasks pages remain capture-closed even when they contain
  deliberately migrated residents.

The Monthly Tasks container must participate in the page's flex layout. A
44px invisible button at the end of a content-sized section does not satisfy
this contract.

## Monthly Calendar — tap the day to write on that day

Dogfooding supersedes 1.5.1 ruling 8's emphasis. On a writable current or past
Calendar, writing on the Calendar is the primary day-row gesture:

1. Tapping the day number/weekday or otherwise blank space in the row opens
   that row's capture panel. The panel is visibly associated with the chosen
   date and offers only Task and Event.
2. The date is derived from the rendered row. No client-authored placement or
   `page_on` chooses residency, and a crafted Note or wrong-month request
   still refuses at the server boundary.
3. Existing resident entry rows and their eligible controls remain directly
   operable; their taps do not reveal capture.
4. Direct Daily Log access remains a separate 44px-or-larger chevron at the
   row edge with the accessible name `Open Daily Log for <date>`. It is a
   secondary navigation gesture rather than what happens when the reader taps
   the date itself.
5. A future Calendar keeps the same residents and Daily links but renders no
   Calendar capture target.
6. Reveal alone writes nothing. A new Calendar resident appears only after an
   explicit `Log`, with the existing exact `monthly_calendar` placement and
   `occurs_on` equal to the chosen row.

Nothing is generated from the Daily Log, from dates on other residents, or
from time passing. This is the book's hand-slotted Calendar translated to a
phone: choose the line, then write on it.

## Future Log — reachable capture, explicit kind, aligned entries

### Capture surface

Every month after the current month retains its heading toggle and gains one
trailing blank reveal button immediately below its rendered entries. The
trailing target spans the writable width and is at least 44px high. Thus:

- an empty month can be opened from its heading or body;
- a populated month can be opened by tapping directly below its last entry;
- current and overdue occupied months remain readable but have neither
  reveal target;
- opening a second month closes the first;
- after success, the row closes and focus returns to the exact control that
  opened it, not merely the first control associated with that panel.

The placement controller must therefore remember the active opener when two
buttons control one panel. It must not infer that relationship from DOM order
after submission.

### Task/Event control

Future capture uses the same shared circular kind controls and vertical form
anatomy as Daily, Monthly Calendar, and Collection capture. It does not add a
native dropdown or a Future-only labelled selector:

```text
[ • ] [ ○ ]
[ day ] [ Rapid log…                         ] [ Log ]
```

- The 44px `•` Task circle is selected initially; the 44px `○` Event circle is
  the other option. Their accessible names remain `Task` and `Event`.
- The controls render through the same shared partial and classes as the other
  log entry forms. The selected option therefore has the same visible accent
  border and color treatment in both themes; Future-specific CSS may not strip
  that border or substitute a different control style.
- Both glyphs use the currently selected hand and the established glyph
  fallback exactly like the other forms.
- `aria-pressed` and the hidden `default_kind` agree after every tap.
- Choosing either option focuses `Rapid log…` without a JavaScript
  exception. The day field remains required and is initially focused when the
  month opens.
- The server continues to refuse Notes and every crafted disallowed kind.

The landed controls are technically buttons, but Future-specific CSS removes
their borders and therefore removes the selected treatment. The Future form
also omits the rapid-log line target that kind selection tries to focus. The
amendment fixes both the visible and operable defects rather than replacing
the shared rapid-log vocabulary.

### Entry alignment

Future residents keep the existing information and order. Within each root
row, day number, signifier, bullet, entry text, and time/meta align on one
baseline at 320px and 390px in every shipped hand. Wrapped text may grow the
row downward without moving the day or bullet to the following visual line.
Nested kept context remains readable and does not flatten into another root.

## Index — blank-page creation gesture

The Index header remains title-first:

```text
Index                         [New Collection]
Your collections
```

The explicit button remains the discoverable and keyboard-reachable control.
After the registered Topic list and `Open by Topic` control, a second visually
blank button fills the remaining canvas above the tabs and opens the same New
Collection panel.

- Tapping a Topic link still opens that Collection.
- Tapping `Open by Topic`, its field, or its Open button never opens New
  Collection.
- The trailing blank region opens only the create panel and focuses Topic.
- Opening either disclosure closes the other under the existing one-panel
  rule.
- Reveal performs no request. Only Create can persist a new, unindexed,
  same-user Collection through the existing controller/model path.
- Empty, populated, create-refused, locate-refused, missing, deleted, and
  tenant-isolated states preserve the 1.5.2 disclosure rules. Unindexed Topics
  are never surfaced as suggestions or automatic discovery.

## Monthly Migration — empty stages still require participation

The queue remains completely derived from current Entry state. This amendment
adds no ritual table, completion flag, session key, cookie, local storage, or
sync field.

### Outgoing checkpoint

`Review outgoing tasks` always opens the outgoing stage. If an eligible task
exists, the current one-at-a-time review is unchanged. If none exists, the
page says:

```text
Review outgoing tasks
No unresolved outgoing tasks.
[Scan the Future Log]
```

It does not redirect past the stage and does not say the ritual is complete.
The explicit link opens the Future stage.

### Future checkpoint

If due Future work exists, the current one-at-a-time Task/Event controls are
unchanged. If none exists, the page says:

```text
Scan the Future Log
Nothing due for September.
[Finish Monthly Migration]
```

The exact month follows the target. Only that explicit link opens the existing
completion URL and state.

### Completion truthfulness

`Monthly Migration complete` appears only on the completion route after the
reader follows Finish (or deliberately opens that URL) and only while both
live candidate relations are empty. The existing canonical safety remains:
new outgoing work redirects completion to outgoing; newly due Future work
redirects it to Future. Reopening from the Monthly Log begins at setup again
because no completion history is persisted.

Resolving the last candidate now lands on its stage's empty checkpoint rather
than declaring completion inside the same action response. Refusals and stale
commands still write nothing and return to the truthful current candidate or
empty checkpoint with the existing alert.

## State and security boundaries

This amendment is presentation and orchestration only:

- no migration, table, column, model, parser, grammar, seed, job, notification,
  or background task;
- no page placement change and no new Entry or Collection command;
- no automatic Calendar, Future, Monthly Tasks, Index, or ritual write;
- no broad search, recent list, suggestion, fuzzy lookup, or automatic Index
  registration;
- no relaxation of current/past/future capture admission;
- no change to append-only `Entry#move_to!`, same-user validation, UUIDv7,
  soft deletion without cascades, one-successor enforcement, immutable
  `page_kind`/`page_on`/`collection_id`, or exact NULL event/note state;
- no writes to dormant `hlc` or `server_seq`.

Server authorization remains the authority. A large reveal target never
makes a hidden or crafted request legal.

## Required tests

Use the existing Rails system lane. Do not introduce Playwright or a second
browser harness for this amendment.

### Real-browser system tests

At 390x844 and 320px, with light/dark and at least two non-sans hands:

1. Assert `Daily Log` has no duplicate date beneath it and the centered day
   navigation still names the viewed page.
2. On an empty Daily page, a one-entry page, a wrapped-entry page, and a page
   with an open action strip, click a real blank coordinate immediately below
   the last rendered content. The form opens and focuses. Assert the reveal
   region reaches the usable space above the tab bar and never overlaps it.
3. Repeat the coordinate-level trailing-space test for current Monthly Tasks;
   prove a future Tasks page has no reveal target.
4. Click the visible Calendar date/blank row, not an aria-selected hidden edge
   button. Assert the exact day's Task/Event form opens, no row exists before
   submit, and the submitted resident appears only on that Calendar page.
   Separately click the Daily chevron and prove it navigates without capture.
5. Open a populated Future month from directly below its last entry. Exercise
   both labelled kind buttons, assert visible/ARIA/hidden-field selection,
   focus, persisted Task/Event kind, success close, and return-to-actual-opener
   behavior.
6. Use rendered geometry to prove Future day, glyph, text, and metadata share
   one row within a small tolerance in both tested hands, including a wrapped
   entry, and prove no horizontal overflow.
7. Open New Collection from Index trailing blank space, then prove Topic,
   locate, registered links, and tab controls do not trigger it. Repeat the
   existing empty/refused/tenant-safe states.
8. With no candidates, walk Setup -> outgoing empty -> Future empty -> Finish
   -> completion and assert completion is absent before Finish. Repeat with
   the last outgoing candidate and last Future candidate so each resolution
   lands on the appropriate empty checkpoint.
9. Keep 44x44 target, keyboard focus, `aria-expanded`, `aria-pressed`, fixed
   tab clearance, no-overflow, theme, and hand audits green on every changed
   surface.

Selector-only clicks on invisible controls do not satisfy flows 2-5. These
tests exist specifically because such clicks passed while the phone gesture
failed.

## Specifier source-alignment clarifications — 2026-08-26

The pre-implementation pass checked this accepted contract against the landed
Daily, Monthly, Future, Index, Entry capture, placement-controller, and Monthly
Migration sources. It found no model, schema, route, parser, library, or
dependency need. These clarifications record how the approved behavior meets
the existing source; they do not reopen either operator decision.

1. **Capture keeps its existing commands.** Daily, Calendar, Monthly Tasks,
   and Future submissions still use the generic `entries#create` path with the
   server-rendered page/date context already used by those views. Index Create
   still uses `collections#create`. The larger buttons only disclose those
   existing forms: their reveal gesture issues no request and supplies no new
   placement, lifecycle, Collection-discovery, or persistence operation.
   Existing controller/domain admission remains authoritative for every
   crafted kind, date, tenant, and page request.
2. **Calendar changes gesture ownership, not placement authority.** The
   server-rendered day continues to supply the existing Calendar form's exact
   date and `monthly_calendar` context. The former day link becomes a capture
   control, and the existing Daily path moves to the separate labelled
   chevron. No new endpoint, redirect parameter, or writable `page_on` field
   is introduced.
3. **Future joins the landed shared form contract.** The shared kind source is
   `entries/_kind_buttons.html.erb`; the shared form anatomy and line target
   are owned by `entries/_rapid_log_form.html.erb` and
   `rapid_log_controller.js`. Future's required day field may extend that
   shared anatomy, but Future must not keep a visually divergent copy. The
   mock's handwritten `O` is the existing display mapping for canonical event
   glyph `○`; `hand_glyph` and the selected hand/fallback stack remain the
   authority, with no Entry glyph or grammar change.
4. **The opener reference is transient DOM state only.** When a heading and a
   trailing button control one Future panel, `placement_controller.js`
   remembers the control used for that opening and returns focus to it after a
   successful submit. It may not recover the opener by taking the first DOM
   match. The same controller continues to enforce the Index's one-panel rule.
   No cookie, session, local storage, URL parameter, or server field records
   either disclosure.
5. **The four existing ritual URLs express the explicit sequence.** An empty
   outgoing GET renders `No unresolved outgoing tasks.` at the existing
   outgoing URL; `Scan the Future Log` links to the existing Future URL. An
   empty Future GET renders `Nothing due for <target month>.` there; `Finish
   Monthly Migration` links to the existing completion URL. A successful
   outgoing or Future resolution redirects back to its own stage, so resolving
   that stage's last candidate reveals its empty checkpoint. The completion
   action retains its live canonicalization: direct access is deliberate but
   redirects to outgoing or Future whenever either candidate relation is no
   longer empty. Explicitness is navigation through these GET states, never a
   persisted acknowledgement token.
6. **Browser evidence comes from Rails.** Coordinate clicks, screenshots,
   rendered geometry, focus, accessibility state, and phone-width overflow are
   exercised through `bin/rails test:system` in headless Chrome. No Codex
   Desktop in-app Browser attempt, fallback, or limitation statement belongs
   in implementation or QA evidence.
