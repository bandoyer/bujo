# Slice 1.4.3 — grammar: dates and times people actually type

> **PARKED, UNBUILT (2026-08-24).** Written, reviewed, and then
> deliberately shelved: the operator ruled that routing entries by
> parsing dates out of prose cuts against the method's heart —
> placement should be a deliberate gesture, not a side effect of
> phrasing. Slice 1.4.1 (placement and capture) is what shipped
> instead. The grammar stays frozen at its 1.1 forms. This spec is
> kept as the record of the road not taken; if the grammar ever does
> expand, start from here.

A parser slice — the first since 1.1 to touch `lib/bujo/`, and the
grammar's one deliberate expansion. It drains most of 1.1's deferred
list, chosen from real dogfooding failures ("haircut on 9/5 @ 12:30
PM" captured as undated text). **The grammar stays closed and
deterministic**: every form below is exact syntax with one meaning;
nothing is fuzzy, locale-dependent, or library-backed. Purity rules
from 1.1 bind unchanged (stdlib `Date` only, no clock access —
relative forms resolve against the `today:` argument; the boundary
test and the grep still stand).

**This spec pre-decides the judgment calls; report gaps instead of
inventing rulings.**

## New forms

### Dates

1. **Numeric month/day**: `M/D` and `MM/DD` — month first, US
   convention, ruled once and forever (no locale option). Resolves
   like `<month> <day>` already does: the smallest real date ≥ today.
   `9/5`, `09/05` parse; `13/5`, `9/32`, `0/5` stay text.
2. **Numeric with year**: `M/D/YYYY` — absolute, past allowed, must
   be a real calendar date. Two-digit years (`9/5/27`) stay text —
   the ambiguity isn't worth three keystrokes.
3. **Year on month-day**: `<month> <day> <YYYY>` (`sep 5 2027`) —
   absolute, past allowed, real dates only. The year token is
   consumed only when it immediately follows a valid month-day and
   is four digits ≥ 1900; `sep 5 400` leaves `400` as text.
4. **Weekday names**: full or 3-letter (`friday`, `fri`,
   case-insensitive) — the smallest date ≥ today falling on that
   weekday (today counts when it matches, same spirit as `today`).
   `next friday` is NOT grammar — colloquial "next" is genuinely
   ambiguous and stays deferred; the weekday covers the coming week,
   explicit dates cover everything else.

### Times

5. **Spaced meridiem**: the existing `H[:MM]am|pm` gains an optional
   single space — `12:30 pm`, `6 pm`, `9:15 AM`. Same normalization
   to 24-hour `"HH:MM"`; same hour/minute validity rules. A meridiem
   with nothing before it (`at pm`) stays text.
6. **`noon` and `midnight`**: `12:00` and `00:00`. Exact words,
   case-insensitive.

### Connectors

7. **`on`, `at`, `@`** are consumed as noise ONLY when the very next
   token(s) parse as a date (for `on`) or time/date (for `at`/`@`) —
   so `haircut on 9/5 @ 12:30 pm` yields text "haircut", date Sep 5,
   time 12:30. A connector followed by anything else is ordinary
   text: `meet at the cafe` and `dinner at 6` (bare `6` is not a
   time token) keep every word. At most one connector is consumed
   per date/time slot; `on at 9/5` keeps `on` as text.

## Unchanged and still deferred

Pipeline order, longest-match-first, tag handling, glyph prefixes,
and every 1.1 acceptance rule stand. Still out, deliberately:
`next <weekday>`, `tonight`, durations, recurrence, date ranges,
2-digit years, day/month locale order, timezone syntax, escape
syntax, i18n, and natural-language relatives ("in two weeks") —
the parse-preview follow-up (separate slice) is the answer to
"what did it understand", not more grammar.

## The examples table (grow 1.1's, don't fork it)

Every new form lands in the 1.1 spec's example-table format with
kind/state/prio/text/date/time/tags columns, including the traps:
`9/5` next-occurrence rollover across a year boundary, `9/32` as
text, `sep 5 400` keeping the number, `friday` on a Friday, `dinner
at 6` untouched, `@ noon`, `12:30 pm` vs `12:30pm` equivalence, and
a connector before a non-date (`on second thought…` — text intact).

## File scope

`lib/bujo/`, `test/lib/`, and `docs/slices/` (this file). **Nothing
else** — no app-layer changes at all; the UI already renders
whatever the parser returns. If an app change seems needed, that is
a finding.

## Quality bars

- The full battery, and above all: **`bundle exec mutant run` on
  `Bujo::RapidLog*` stays at 100.00% killed** — the count will grow
  well past 1105 and every new mutant dies. This bar is the slice.
- The purity boundary test and the `Date.today|Time\.|DateTime|ENV`
  grep stay clean.
- CRAP ≤ 6 holds on the parser despite the new branches — decompose
  rather than pile conditionals.
- Round-trip property from 1.1 (parse → render → parse) still holds
  for every new form.
